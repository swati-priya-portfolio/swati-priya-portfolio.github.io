01 / THE PROBLEM

PARENTS CAME TO PAY SCHOOL FEES. BUT FIRST, THEY HAD TO NAVIGATE A FINANCIAL APPLICATION.

Choosing a plan was only the beginning. Parents then had to provide personal and financial details, complete checks, and authorise repayments. Each stage introduced another question about money, information, or what came next.

THREE KEY MOMENTS IN THE JOURNEY
01 / CHOOSING A PLAN

What do I pay today and every month?

Payable today, monthly instalments, duration and applicable fees needed to be easy to compare.

02 / VERIFICATION

Why are these details needed now?

Sharing financial details needed an explanation of the check and a route forward if it failed.

03 / REPAYMENT SETUP

It says pending. What should I do?

Payment success, repayment setup and application review needed to communicate different states.

Each step asked parents to act. The journey needed to explain why.

My focus was to make each commitment understandable—and make the next action clear.

03 / DESIGN CHALLENGE

HELP PARENTS CHOOSE WITH CONFIDENCE.
THEN HELP THEM FINISH.

Two priorities guided the journey: make the financial commitment understandable, and make the next required action visible.

01 / CHOOSE

Make payment terms clear

Show payable today, monthly instalments, duration and any applicable fee in a scannable breakdown.

02 / COMPLETE

Make every next step clear

Explain why details are needed, show progress, and give pending or failed steps a clear next action. Preserve completed work wherever possible.

REQUIREMENTS → EARLY FLOWS

I gathered requirements with product managers and spoke with parents and institutes. I explored ideas through flowcharts and low-fidelity wireframes, then reviewed the sequence with the PM and CEO.

FEEDBACK → FINAL UI

Stakeholder feedback guided further research. I studied banking journeys to understand similar checks, refined the flow and screens with the PM and CEO, and handed the designs to developers.

15 / DESIGN RESPONSE

WHAT THE DESIGN RESOLVES.

Three decisions balance comparison, commitment and progress. Each leaves a specific question to validate.

COMPARE

“Which plan works for me?”

MAKE PLANS COMPARABLE

A lower EMI can mean a longer commitment. Amount and duration support comparison; total cost and fees also need to be clear before commitment.

PAYMENT

“What do I pay today?”

SHOW THE IMMEDIATE COST

The amount due today belongs before payment-method selection. Parents still need to understand how that payment relates to future instalments.

STATUS

“Am I actually done?”

SEPARATE ACTION FROM REVIEW

Payment, repayment setup and approval are distinct milestones. A pending state needs to explain whose action comes next.

16 / REFLECTION

CLARITY DOESN’T END
AT PAYMENT.

MY DESIGN TAKEAWAY

The key distinction is between paying now, authorising future repayments and receiving approval. The interface needs to explain each milestone separately.

Every step needs an explanation.

The design constraint: identity checks, account verification and repayment authorisation each need their own explanation.
